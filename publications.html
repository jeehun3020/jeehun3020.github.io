DR-SCA: Directional Encoder Blocks and Semantic-Conditioned Coordinate Attention for UAV Semantic Segmentation

Authors
Jihoon Jeong, Wangduk Seo*

Journal
Electronics 2026, 15(19), 4396 (MDPI) · Published 24 Sept. 2026 · doi:10.3390/electronics15194396

Abstract

Urban UAV scenes exhibit strong directional structure, and their foreground composition varies substantially across frames. DR-SCA is a compact RepVGG-style encoder–decoder that combines Directional RepVGG (D-Rep) encoder blocks, whose horizontal and vertical training branches fold into one deployed 3×3 convolution, with bottleneck Semantic-Conditioned Coordinate Attention (SCA), which modulates the Coordinate Attention descriptor with a global descriptor from the same feature. Under a shared ten-seed, 50-epoch protocol trained from scratch, DR-SCA reaches 61.75 ± 0.30% mIoU on the UAVid validation split with 2.050M parameters, the highest among six compared methods, and also ranks first on UDD6 and LoveDA. Baselines with ImageNet-pretrained encoders narrow this margin and match or exceed DR-SCA on the official UAVid test split.

Learning Concentration System Based on Facial and Behavioral Analysis in Intelligent Study Rooms

Authors
Jihoon Jeong, Sieun Kim, Yeeun Kim, Eunseo Park, Seunga Seo, Seoryeong Jang, Kiyeon Ham

Conference
Proceedings of the KIIT Summer Conference

Year
2025

Abstract

This paper presents a vision-based learning concentration analysis system for intelligent study environments. The proposed system analyzes facial expressions and behavioral cues such as eye closure, yawning, head movement, and seat departure to quantitatively evaluate learner focus levels.

Content Verification System Based on Multimodal AI Consistency

Authors
Jihoon Jeong*, Sanghyeon Kim**, Hyeonjin Kim*, Jiho Ryu*, Jeongah Yoon*, Wonseok Lee*, Jinyoung Lee*

Conference
Proceedings of the KIIT Summer Conference

Year
2026

Recognition
Best Paper Award (Silver), First Author, Advanced Capstone Top Excellence Award

Abstract

Existing AI-generated content detection methods have not sufficiently judged semantic discrepancies between modals because they analyze images, videos, and texts individually. Subsequent studies have developed frequency-based image detection, frame and time axis-based image detection, and language model-based text detection methods, and the detection performance has improved in each modal. In this paper, we propose a system that combines single modal detection results and multimodal consistency analysis to improve the accuracy and interpretability of AI-generated content verification.
